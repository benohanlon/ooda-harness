# Synthetic role walkthrough: Chief of Staff / Perception Check

**Synthetic document walkthrough.** Every source card, participant, event and result below is invented to exercise the proposed structure. This is a bounded example of the seven-section template, not a complete Chief of Staff contract, an appointment, an actual run, a saved checkpoint or an observed outcome. The [accepted reusable CoS reference](../roles/chief-of-staff.md) supplies the role contract for private specialisation. This walkthrough establishes no runtime evidence; assess actual commissioned operation separately.

Read the [ecology of mind](../docs/ecology-of-mind.md), [common operating contract](../docs/operating-contract.md), [role template](../roles/ROLE-TEMPLATE.md) and [Perception Check method](../skills/perception-check/SKILL.md) as the maintained definitions. The example supplies only the domain and assignment particulars.

## 1. Purpose and accountability

In this fixture, Chief of Staff helps the sovereign decide what needs attention. Its contribution is a coherent picture of priorities, commitments, material unknowns and the next useful moves, with evidence the sovereign can use. Talent retains responsibility for career assessment, workflows and case records. Technical diagnosis remains with Engineering when commissioned.

The fictional maintained brief defines two relevant shared measures: sovereign minutes spent reconstructing or correcting work, and commitments resolved with evidence of the agreed result. The former is an effort measure; the latter is an outcome measure. A sourced report or prepared next move indicates progress. Source counts and agent exchanges are activity. The brief owns baselines, targets, timeframe, definitions and results. This example supplies no measured improvement or numeric target and does not impose these measures on the actual harness.

Chief of Staff reports to the sovereign for this direct commission. A bounded collector reports to its assigning participant. The task record is the sole continuation owner.

## 2. Responsibilities and domain ownership

Chief of Staff owns synthesis and coordination for the check. It reads maintained records, identifies decisions and unfinished work, explains coverage and leaves a usable continuation. It preserves other owners' decisions and the distinction between proposed and authorised action.

| Responsibility | Useful result and recipient | Maintained owner or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current orientation | Prioritised findings and decisions for the sovereign | Current brief and this task record |
| Career case awareness | Surface a pending decision without redoing Talent's assessment | Talent case owner |
| Schedule awareness | Explain configured work and observed execution separately | Scheduler configuration and run-record owners |
| Continuation | Recoverable result, exact remainder and next owner | Existing task record |

## 3. Operating model and execution

The bounded commission is: “Run a Perception Check over the past 72 hours. Include earlier unresolved commitments. Read the supplied sources, prepare the report and save it to this task record. No external sends or schedule changes.”

Two hypothetical participant contexts, A and B, receive the same relevant model specification, capabilities, accepted guidance, contract, brief and source access. A initially acts as doer/giver and is the single report-write owner. B acts as observer/active receiver. Each retains a complete OODA and can investigate or propose the next move. Their starting foundation is checked before relying on their contributions.

The effort boundary is one check of the supplied cards, one targeted prior-work retrieval and one report/checkpoint. A meaningful exchange occurs before conclusions are settled. An unavailable source is reported as a coverage gap. A decision beyond the brief returns to the sovereign while independent authorised work continues. The check ends with delivery and receipt of the bounded report; it does not wait for every downstream outcome.

## 4. Capabilities, methods and knowledge routes

Both participants use the Perception Check method to establish horizon, actual coverage, observations, useful findings and continuation. They use [Writing and Editing](../skills/writing-and-editing/SKILL.md) and its [editorial standard](../skills/writing-and-editing/references/EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md) for the report. [Sense of Taste](../skills/sense-of-taste/SKILL.md) supports bounded review against F1–F5 while specialist procedures retain their own acceptance checks.

| Trigger | Retrieval and useful contribution | Owner and application state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need an earlier report or accepted solution | Recover F4 with its original task and later correction F5, following the [common retrieval rules](../docs/operating-contract.md#bounded-workers-and-prior-work-retrieval) | Prior task owner; distinguish retrieved, assessed and applied |
| Need to interpret the career case | Read F2 and current steering from F1, without turning a preference into a new filter | Talent case and sovereign decision owners |
| Need to interpret apparent missed work | Compare F3 configuration, visible receipts and known coverage limits | Scheduler/run owners; inaccessible execution remains unknown |

The crawler is a bounded retrieval contribution. It can be a tool rather than an agent. Its brief asks for the earlier report, original scope and linked later corrections, with source labels, coverage and omissions. It receives no write or send authority. B owns receipt and review of this return, including its filters; A owns integration into the report. If the retrieval does not return within the agreed effort, B reports the gap and the pair decides whether the evidence supports a narrower report or requires a decision from the sovereign. A returned link alone is insufficient.

## 5. Authority, delegation and handoffs

A owns the report write to the task record. B can investigate the permitted cards, draft and challenge. Both may commission a worker within the agreed effort and parallelism limits. No source-card access grants outreach, a new role appointment, schedule changes or automatic reruns.

The report returns career decisions to the sovereign and proposed technical investigation to its existing owner. Those proposals do not commission new work. A polarity change can let B draft a revised passage while A receives and assesses it. A retains write ownership unless the assignment explicitly transfers it.

## 6. Records, completion and continuity

F1 and the task record own current steering, this check's result and continuation. F2–F5 remain with their original owners. The report refers to them rather than creating another career queue or schedule register. Private runtime bindings would be supplied separately in a real assignment.

A checkpoint in this fixture would retain:

- Accepted F1 revision and guidance routes; A as doer/write owner and B as active receiver, with any later polarity change.
- F2 decision surfaced without reopening its completed assessment; F3 execution unknown; F4 and F5 retrieved and applied to correct the report.
- A's initial failure inference and B's reason for rejecting it; B's proposed hybrid exclusion and A's source-based rejection. Both corrections reach the revised artifact.
- Prepared report revision, delivery/receipt status, remaining evidence gap, next owner and any uncertain effects.
- Next move: deliver the report if still pending, then await the sovereign's decision. Verify the exact task record before retrying an uncertain write.

A fresh context would recover F1, the guidance, source routes, both material perspectives and the exact remainder, checking newer steering before acting. If B were unavailable, the record would identify degraded paired operation and what requires restoration. This describes the recovery requirement; this walkthrough has not performed a replacement or verified persistence.

## 7. Review and learning

The report must preserve the hybrid case's eligibility and completed assessment, avoid an unsupported execution verdict, distinguish proposals from decisions, and expose coverage. Evaluation uses the brief's measures and relevant qualitative judgement. It includes damaging criticism as well as missed defects.

The task owner retains material findings and their dispositions. F5 stays with the earlier task's owner and retrieval trigger. A report repair applies the accepted method within the current aims. A proposal to change those aims or the measures returns to the sovereign. Later usefulness and effort can only be assessed from actual work and feedback, including a fresh analogous case.

### Reciprocal work before the report

1. **A proposes an initial picture.** The pending career decision needs attention. A also infers that the enabled collection failed because F3 shows no receipt.
2. **B investigates during the work.** It notices that the view may have limited coverage and commissions the bounded retrieval. The returned F4 includes its original task and F5. B identifies the prior error and proposes classifying execution as unknown, with the exact missing receipt/coverage evidence named. A checks F3 and F5, accepts the correction and preserves the sound pending-decision finding.
3. **B makes a mistaken criticism.** It proposes excluding the hybrid case to maximise freedom of action. A recovers the actual wording in F2 and explains that preference affects priority without removing eligibility. B withdraws that criticism. The case remains in the report with its decision still pending.
4. **They apply the contributions.** B drafts the corrected schedule passage and A checks it against the originals. A writes the coherent report. This local reversal of emphasis does not silently move write authority. The saved record would identify what each contribution changed and whether delivery was received.

Both participants investigate and revise their orientation before the result is settled. While B awaits the retrieval, A can continue assessing the career decision against F1–F2. An early finding can redirect the remaining inquiry within the agreed scope and effort. The receiver contributes a useful next search; the doer corrects the receiver's interpretation. Neither must yield because of its hat. Their agreement still cannot establish F3's actual execution or supply F6's outside response.

### What this walkthrough can expose

If the crawler returned only F4, both agents could reinforce the same false premise. If they searched only remote cases, both could miss F2. A silent final checker would miss the chance to redirect the investigation. Habitual yielding could lose a valid correction, while repeated criticism could damage sound work or consume the effort budget. The source cards make these errors inspectable without making this dialogue a compulsory script.

A real trial must establish two actual contexts, received and applied contributions, useful delivery, interruption/replacement and recovery. It must compare quality and whole effort against a capable single agent and test a saved correction on fresh work under the [runtime acceptance conditions](../docs/operating-contract.md#runtime-acceptance). This example establishes no comparative benefit.
