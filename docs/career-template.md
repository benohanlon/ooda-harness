# Career direction template

Use this to create or revise the maintained `career.md` in a [private Sovereign Profile](sovereign-profile-template.md). It gives an assigned career agent a common starting point for assessing opportunities and choosing evidence. Link the completed file from the private profile index and the private role application or current brief. During a career assignment, follow that route to the completed file and retrieve the approved résumé, full cases or live opportunity records when the question needs them.

Start from the sovereign’s actual account, approved materials and original evidence. Reuse existing records and include only sections useful to the work. Keep detailed cases, current pursuits and routine application answers with their maintained owners. Follow the [Writing and Editorial Standards](../skills/writing-and-editing/references/EDITORIAL-STANDARDS.md) and [Career writing guidance](../skills/writing-and-editing/references/career-writing.md).

## Write the origin story

Open with the work the person did and why it mattered. Use their own language. Keep the earlier experience that explains their choices. Follow one chain of cause and effect through the work they owned, what it made possible, the consequential decision or result, and how their present work builds on it. Organise paragraphs around turns in that story.

Put proof at the turn it substantiates. A before-and-after measure, dated adoption result or recorded decision can establish scale and consequence. Preserve who acted, who decided and what the measure actually covers. Use the underlying evidence when making consequential claims. Link sources for later verification and keep the story readable on its own. [The Anatomy of Story](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781429923705/theanatomyofstory/) · [The Moth’s storytelling guidance](https://www.themoth.org/tell-your-story/storytelling-tips-tricks)

## Write the career thesis

The career thesis says what the next move should add. In `career.md`, give each active direction a short summary that tells the reader where to look, what outcome the sovereign would own and what signal makes the opportunity timely. Link a separate card when the full thesis is useful on its own, for example when an agent needs its search criteria or a person needs to revisit its evidence. Keep the full thesis with its maintained owner and link each card back to the parent. When testing a direction, compare it with dated feedback from a recruiter, colleague or hiring team. Record what their response established in the existing case. A lack of feedback leaves the thesis untested.

### Copyable thesis card

```markdown
# <Career direction>

<Name> speaks in the first person in the Career Thesis and Executive Search Brief. Use these sections to judge fit. Follow the Sourcing Plan and its search parameters to run the search.

## Career Thesis

<In the person's voice: what they believe about this field, why they want to work in it, what problem they can solve and what the next move should let them own.>

## Executive Search Brief

<Make this section independently shareable. Link the approved public résumé, professional profile and relevant work.>

**Target organisations and hiring situation.** <Which employers should a recruiter think of? What change makes a call timely?>
**Mandate.** <Outcome, likely titles, sponsor, scope, team and decision rights.>
**Relevant track record.** <One or two attributable results linked to the hiring problem. If the evidence comes from another field, say what the person can carry across and what remains unproved.>
**Working arrangements and remuneration.** <Include the practical terms the person wants recruiters to see, using the parent career brief as the source.>

## Sourcing Plan

Write this section for workers that already have a source assignment. Describe the evidence that matters to this thesis in each loop: changes at known targets, newly discovered vacancies or trigger events, and opportunities to develop an existing relationship. Give examples specific enough to distinguish an observed fact from a plausible hiring need. Specify what the search lead needs to recognise relevance, uncertainty and repeated sightings across sources. The job brief supplies the source, permitted actions and return destination. The search lead joins repeated sightings to one case, retains each relevant thesis and identifies the outcome the hire would own. Bring back what changed the assessment, who could substantiate it and the event or date for another look.

| Loop | Evidence worth returning for this thesis |
| --- | --- |
| Closed | <A material change since the known record and what it changes.> |
| Open | <A fresh vacancy or dated event, the implied work and what remains to be confirmed.> |
| Network | <Who matters, the relationship history, what the candidate could offer and why now.> |

## Search Parameters

| Search group | Agreed terms | How to use them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products and markets | <Primary terms and spelling variants> | <How the product or market can be verified.> |
| Organisations | <Employer types, operating entities and common descriptions> | <Where to look for the actual hiring organisation.> |
| Functions | <Functions that might own the outcome> | <When the responsibilities establish a fit.> |
| Outcomes and participants | <What changes and for whom> | <Which terms retrieve opportunities and which help qualify them.> |
| Adjacent vocabulary | <Broader sector, mission or culture language> | <Where these terms are useful and which false positives to expect.> |
| Title variants | <Seniority group> AND <function group>, plus common acronyms | <Illustrate with employer-used titles. Include unfamiliar titles with equivalent scope.> |

For title discovery, list seniority terms and function terms as independent groups. Combine any seniority with any function in either order. Include relevant abbreviations, a worked example and a keyword-only fallback. Exact title examples illustrate the pattern. Retain a route for relevant roles whose scope appears only in the responsibilities.

State the source-independent combination rules, including broad discovery and targeted searches at known employers. Translate them into supported controls in each source worker. For a feed sorted by original posting date, page to the cutoff or the end of the feed and assess the pooled records. A relevance-ranked or recently updated feed can contain older records before newer ones. Use a supported date filter or exhaust the results before reporting complete coverage. For a keyword service, compare a broad pass with a targeted pass on the same records where possible. Check one expected match and inspect the irrelevant results before narrowing the terms.

| Parameter | Worker decision |
| --- | --- |
| Recency and availability | <Window, cutoff, timezone and evidence of the original posting or dated material change. Record first sighting separately.> |
| Relevance and timing | <Product, owned outcome, trigger and evidence of commitment.> |
| Authority and practical fit | <Refer to the brief. Distinguish hard constraints from unresolved preferences.> |
| Evidence and history | <Original source, stable identity and previous decisions.> |
| Coverage | <Revision, queries, filters, stopping condition and remaining work. Distinguish a complete zero from a partial run with no matches so far.> |

Give one high-signal example for each loop and a plausible false positive. Preserve the difference between a verified role, an inferred mandate and a relationship move. Return through the assigned destination.

```

Keep source credentials, schedules and platform-specific calls with the worker. Keep candidate details, private cases and search vocabulary in the person's own card. Check that the brief makes sense when copied alone and that two workers reading the same revision can explain why the same vacancy qualifies.

## Copyable career file

```markdown
# Career direction

<Describe what this file owns and link the current approved résumé, practical answers and evidence at their maintained homes.>

## Problems this person solves and what they want to own

<State the problems, the way they work, the outcomes they own and the authority and resources their next remit needs.>

## Origin story

<Open with what they built or changed and the work they personally owned. Include earlier experience that explains it and evidence of scale or stakes.>

<Show what that work made possible, who acted or decided and what resulted. Put the strongest verified proof alongside the event it explains.>

<Connect that result to their current work. Include what exists or has actually happened today.>

## Career message house

**Main message.** <One sentence about the contribution a recipient should understand.>

| Supporting message | How it works | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <A distinct supporting claim> | <What the person does to produce the outcome> | <Verified result and link to the underlying evidence> |

## Career thesis

<State what the next move should add. Summarise each active direction and link the maintained thesis card when it has its own address.>

## Working arrangements and remuneration

<Record forms of work, location, travel, working pattern, remuneration and availability. Preserve preferences, firm requirements and agreed exceptions. Link the maintained answer when it already exists.>

## Cases and proof

<Group approved materials and the strongest cases by the questions they answer. Say what each case shows and link to the version suitable for the intended audience. Keep routine application answers in the FAQ and full examples with their maintained owners.>
```

## Career thesis references and worked examples

When drafting or reviewing a thesis card, use the examples for the section you are working on.

- **Career thesis.** Allison Mnookin explains how she tested her account of her strengths with former colleagues and used it to recognise an unexpected opportunity. Stacey Feero shows an excerpt from her own candidate one-pager. [Mnookin’s firsthand account](https://womenpm.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Allison-H.-Mnookin-_-Phyl-Terry.pdf) · [Feero’s worked example](https://nofearproductleadership.substack.com/p/interviewing-for-your-next-product)
- **Executive search brief.** Pages 7–11 of Spencer Stuart’s OHSU president specification connect the institution’s needs to outcomes, authority and evidence of capability. For a candidate’s brief, describe the situation in which a recruiter should call. [OHSU position specification](https://www.ohsu.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/OHSU-President-51221-015-SPC-Final.pdf#page=7) · [Spencer Stuart’s candidate guidance](https://www.spencerstuart.com/research-and-insight/practical-advice-for-experienced-job-seekers-a-reactive-and-proactive-mix)
- **Sourcing plan.** Isaacson, Miller’s sample plan shows how a first review brings together prospects and lessons from conversations. Craig Elias and Tibor Shanto’s *SHiFT!* explains how a trigger event can change timing. Ask what changed and what evidence would confirm a mandate. Duke’s candidate-side worksheet distinguishes interest in an employer, a potential advocate and evidence of hiring. [Sample search work plan, p. 124](https://fa.ucf.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2016/11/C00001706-Isaacson-Miller-Inc.pdf#page=124) · [Duke’s employer target list](https://sites.duke.edu/prattgsps/career-communications-team/job-internship-search/understand-the-job-market-employers/target-list/)
- **Search parameters.** Cochrane’s worked searches show how to combine alternatives within a concept and require evidence across concepts. Check the provider’s documentation when translating that logic into supported filters, sorting and pagination. [Cochrane search methods, §3.4](https://training.cochrane.org/chapter04-tech-supplonlinepdfv65270924#page=66) · [Himalayas job search parameters](https://himalayas.app/docs/remote-jobs-mcp)
