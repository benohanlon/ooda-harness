# Short-film workflow

**Draft workflow MVP.** Use this method to develop a short film from a brief, a coherent cast and a small set of shots. The method has been exercised through an initial motion test and a timed rough edit in one production. Consistent finished shots, sound, release and reuse on a second film remain to be demonstrated. Saving this document does not install a skill or update a runtime.

## Scope and ownership

Use this when an assignment calls for a complete short film or a sequence of animated shots. An isolated image or motion experiment can use the relevant production tool directly. The assigned producer owns this work within the existing [role responsibilities](../ROLES.md#agreed-responsibilities); this workflow creates no new role or approval gate.

Apply [Writing and Editing](../skills/writing-and-editing/SKILL.md) to scripts, captions and the production guide. Use the existing [Sense of Taste](../skills/sense-of-taste/SKILL.md) method when a consequential creative review will help. The selected media tools retain their own execution requirements. Project records own the brief, cast, facts, permissions, spending authority and release decisions.

The working deliverable is one recoverable edit of the whole film. Keep private project evidence and media in the project's chosen store, with links from its current checkpoint. The reusable harness holds the method and synthetic examples.

## 1. Establish what the viewer should get

Write a short brief covering:

- The intended audience, channel, viewing size, aspect ratio and rough duration.
- What the viewer should understand or feel, and any action they should take.
- Exact wording that must survive revision, plus the offer and destination when the brief calls for them.
- Current decisions, proposals, unresolved facts and any limit on generation or spending.

Choose one simple story with a visible action and payoff. Put supporting explanations into later clips or accompanying copy when they crowd the film. Record a beat in terms of its job for the viewer before choosing camera moves.

## 2. Recover the cast and visual rules

Retrieve selected character references, brand assets and the latest accepted storyboard before making new artwork. Identify what each input controls: character identity, pose, composition, scenery, drawing style or lighting. A style reference does not replace an identity reference.

Record the project's visual rules. For example, drawn characters can inhabit photographic scenery, with soft near-foreground foliage. Those choices belong to that production, rather than becoming a rule for every film.

Use large character references to build new shot frames. Small storyboard faces help communicate a pose but can carry earlier distortions. Preserve their original source and selection status.

Plan only the layers the shot needs: scenery, cast, foreground occlusion, shadows, logo, captions and sound. State which layers actually exist. A flattened frame with masked repairs is a layered retrofit, not recovered clean scenery, a complete character cutout or a rig.

## 3. Make the whole film visible early

Give each shot an identifier, story job, framing, action and proposed duration. Assemble a rough edit, also called an animatic, from thumbnails, stills and any useful existing motion. Label placeholders clearly.

Keep one master timeline. Vary shot lengths according to action and reading time. Trim a generated take to the edit rather than letting the generator's duration decide the film's rhythm. Give important closing copy, including any invitation, enough time to read at the intended viewing size.

For a visually led film, review the complete cut without narration first. Check whether its intended action and payoff are understandable. For a campaign, also check the audience's useful role, offer and next step. Add scratch dialogue or sound early when the story depends on it. Revise missing or confusing beats. The rough cut is a timing proposal, not finished animation.

An isolated motion study can precede the animatic when it answers a specific feasibility question. Record that sequence honestly, then build the whole edit before committing to the remaining shots.

## 4. Test a bounded shot

Choose the most consequential uncertainty, such as a character turn, two-person interaction or foreground concealment. Inspect a full-size starting frame against the selected references before animating it.

Keep a compact shot brief:

| Item | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Story and action | What happens, what the viewer gets, framing and duration |
| Inputs | Exact files or stable asset IDs and each input's role |
| Revision boundary | Requested change and protected composition, cast, scenery, wardrobe and copy |
| Layers and output | Existing separation, intended dimensions and viewing size |
| Acceptance | Visible traits and action that decide whether the shot works |

For each retained attempt, save the starting frame, exact prompt, exposed model/settings, original output, tool job link, observed cost when available, subsequent edits and review. State unexposed settings as unknown. Retaining a prompt does not guarantee that a hosted service can reproduce an identical render.

Inspect the movement across the clip, especially faces during turns, hands, fur or hair, clothing, logos, camera drift, occlusion and the final frame. Audition sound separately. A visual review cannot establish that the audio works.

## 5. Replace placeholders and revise locally

Insert useful takes into the master timeline and review their effect on the whole film. Preserve screen direction, action continuity and the established visual treatment.

Change the failed shot or part while retaining sound work. Keep previous selected versions recoverable. Compare a bounded revision with its declared protected elements at both detail size and delivery size.

Keep precise typography, logos, changing figures and destinations in native editable overlays where possible. Check source-dependent copy against the project's current owner before release. A liked image can contain outdated wording.

Distinguish four conclusions: a tool produced a file; the file passed named checks; a human selected it; distribution was authorised. Selection of a take does not silently replace a character master.

## 6. Finish, deliver and leave a restart point

Replace remaining placeholders, refine typography and add sound that supports the action and cuts. Inspect the entire exported film at its intended size. Check duration, image quality, audio, copy, destination and any release conditions that actually apply.

Retain the editable timeline, exact selected media, source references and final export. A new context should be able to locate them without reconstructing the chat.

Update the project's existing checkpoint with the current cut, selected sources, demonstrated checks, open issues and next authorised action. Record changes to the method with its maintained owner after they have been tested on the next relevant work.

## Minimum project record

Use existing project folders rather than creating a competing library. A small production needs:

- A brief and current checkpoint.
- Exact shared asset references and their selection status.
- Shot briefs and retained generation attempts.
- One versioned timeline, preview and selected export.
- Review evidence and the next unfinished step.

Keep the record proportional to the shot. It can be a few readable files. Reuse a working editor or assembly tool before building a new pipeline.

## Checks for the next MVP exercise

1. **Resume and rebuild:** a fresh context locates the selected inputs, identifies unfinished placeholders and reconstructs the rough edit using declared dependencies and authorised access. Record any missing input or private dependency.
2. **Revise without regression:** change one expression or action and compare the output with the specified protected elements. Retain failed attempts and the actual visual judgement.
3. **Understand the complete film:** a reviewer without the brief watches at the intended size and describes what they understood and felt. Use a muted cut for a visual story, or scratch audio when sound carries meaning. Compare with the brief's intended understanding, feeling or action. For a participation campaign, check the viewer's useful role, offer and next step. Record confusion before generating further shots.

These are proposed checks for useful reuse. They are not claims that this workflow has passed all three or that it produces viral reach.

## Practice adapted from existing work

- [Blender Studio's Project Gold storyboarding](https://studio.blender.org/blog/project-gold-storyboarding-in-blender/) and [editorial guidance](https://studio.blender.org/tools/pipeline-overview/pre-production/editorial): thumbnails to an animatic, then progressive replacement inside the edit.
- [3Blue1Brown's production instructions](https://github.com/3b1b/videos/blob/306a1346a356c7a7b097875ceb51c5cd67d0a56c/CLAUDE.md): reusable characters/components and previewing. Creator-local scripts and external media paths are not a portable pipeline.
- [Official Comfy workflow templates](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/workflow_templates/blob/main/README.md): retain recipes, dependencies and previews. Adapt the record-keeping to the tool actually used.
- [Baoyu's comic method](https://github.com/JimLiu/baoyu-skills/blob/main/skills/baoyu-comic/SKILL.md): separate identity and style references, retain prompts and revise selected pages or shots. Session reuse is not a proven identity lock; precise text stays editable.
- [Penpot's contribution guidance](https://github.com/penpot/penpot-files/blob/be9b1c33ff7546f2a5246b4c52a96f1183c2fe35/CONTRIBUTING.md): retain editable sources, provenance and explicit selection.

These are adapted practices, not imported toolchains. The production that informed this draft retains the private evidence, source-access limits and actual results with its own checkpoint.
