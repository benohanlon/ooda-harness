## Meaning and sources

Odysseus supplies the engineer's chosen archetype. The Phaeacian ship is the metaphor for a harness carrying the sovereign's intent toward their destination. The distant island represents Ithaca. A small thread of hearth smoke expresses homecoming. Wilson's Book 1 names smoke from his homeland rather than his own hearth specifically. [Literary and visual sources](#earlier-visual-selection).

## Production and continuity

The original 5 November 2026 illustration was generated with the built-in image tool from the sovereign's supplied woodcut reference, then edited to add the dwelling and smoke. Its warm-paper, oxblood and vermilion version remains in Git history. This is a poetic illustration, not an archaeological reconstruction or reproduction of an identified historical print.

The new image-tool edit preserves the square sail, crew, stormy sea, original cut-line texture, distant Ithaca and smoke. Petrol-teal shadows and mint highlights replace warm paper; restrained orange-red preserves the ship's red cheek. Live type supplies the role and the proposed line 'Find a way home.' That is role copy, not a quotation from Homer or Wilson. The card uses the Engineering role label; no further pack number is assigned.

The [brand guidelines](../../brand/GUIDELINES.md) own the common visual system. Artwork changes do not alter the role's contract, source binding or installation.

### Literary bearings

- [Homer, Iliad 2, passage beginning at 631](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerIliad2.html) identifies Odysseus's fleet of twelve ships with vermilion prows. [The Greek lexicon entry](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/%CE%BC%CE%B9%CE%BB%CF%84%CE%BF%CF%80%CE%AC%CF%81%CE%B7%CE%BF%CF%82) explains the red-cheeked epithet through red-painted bows. This supports a visible red prow, rather than requiring a red sail. It does not establish a special mechanical capability.
- [Homer, Odyssey 8, passage beginning at 555](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HomerOdyssey8.html) describes the Phaeacian ships as understanding people's thoughts and travelling without ordinary pilots or steering oars. These are the hosts' ships, distinct from Odysseus's original fleet. That is a particularly useful literary analogy for a harness that carries human intent, provided the distinction remains explicit.
- Treat wine-dark sea as the chosen poetic visual bearing. Deep purple, black-blue and muted oxblood can convey it. This palette is an artistic interpretation, not a claim that one English colour resolves Homer's language.

### Approved visual direction

Odysseus remains the engineer's archetype. The Phaeacian vessel is the proposed image of the harness itself, carrying the sovereign's intent towards their chosen destination. This is our application of the literary passage. It does not make Odysseus the builder of the Phaeacians' ships or transfer their properties to his original fleet.

An original horizontal illustration can use the supplied woodcut reference's rhythmic waves and strong ship silhouette, a visibly red-painted bow and a wine-dark sea. Keep the crew and rigging legible enough to suggest a working vessel. A distant bearing or opening in the weather gives the journey direction. The original generated composition was selected by the sovereign on 5 November 2026. The requested refinement adds a small thread of hearth-smoke above Ithaca. ### Smoke over Ithaca

Emily Wilson's Book 1 has Athena describe Odysseus longing for smoke from his homeland. The [checked text, PDF page 4](https://static.catapult.co/production/class_syllabus_files/872/original/Wilson_Odyssey_Book_1_1664923052.pdf#page=4) supports that motif. It does not specify his own hearth. The small dwelling and smoke in the cover interpret the passage visually. No quotation is embedded in the image.
